# P1 — Measuring stick (PRD E5): build plan

Planner: nami · 2026-11-19 · branch `v2/verified-lab-intelligence` · PRD refs: §3, §6, §7 E5, §11.3, App. A/E

This plan is the contract for P1. Builders implement exactly this; anything ambiguous takes the more
conservative reading and gets a DECISIONS line. Sections are ordered so a builder can read top to
bottom: facts → design → data → rules → harness → CI → tasks → tests → gate → decisions.

---

## 0. Scope

P1 delivers FR-5.1 to FR-5.6:
- a deterministic generator (`tools/eval/generate.py`);
- the renderings;
- ground truth (GT) per report;
- the dev, test and heldout splits;
- the harness (`tools/eval/run.py`, `make eval`) computing M1–M7 per split and per rendering;
- the CI `eval-gate` job and a nightly full run.

Hard rules for P1:
1. **No product change.** P1 commits touch nothing under `app/` or `clinical_reference.py`. The harness observes the current product through a documented adapter (§6.3). Engine fixes belong to P3.
2. **The baseline is recorded as measured.** It is expected to fail most §3 targets (P1 gate text). Nothing in P1 may be adjusted to make the engine look better: not the GT, the degradations, the matching or the thresholds.
3. **GT is clinically derived.** GT comes from the generator's own clinical tables (§3.4, §3.5), never from engine output or engine code. This is enforced by an import test (GN-11).
4. **Heldout hygiene is enforced in code**, not only by convention (§6.11).

Out of scope: the review flow (P2), engine and template fixes (P3), FHIR/HL7 (P4), and photo upload through the API route (logged for P2, see §1 fact 6).

### 2.2 Dependency rule

GT-side and judge-side modules never import `app`, `clinical_reference` or `tools.eval.adapters`. Only `tools/eval/adapters/*` imports `app`, and only lazily, inside adapter construction. `run.py` reaches the adapters through the registry. This is enforced by GN-11 (an AST scan).

### 2.3 Data flow

1. `generate.plan_corpus` builds the corpus plan: per-report seeds, family, split, scenario and patient.
2. `materialize` lays out each report. Layout yields a `PrintedReport`; GT rows are derived from the same `PrintedRow` specs plus the patient truth, so the page and the GT cannot diverge.
3. `materialize` then writes the GT JSON and renders the native file, and the scan, photo and rot90 renderings on demand.
4. `run.py` builds `EvalJob`s from the manifest (§6.1). Each job goes through `adapter.extract` → `match.align` → `review_sim.simulate` → `scorer.score` → `metrics.job_record`.
5. Job records are summed into additive cell counts per (split, rendering). The gate and the report are computed from those counts.

Further rules:
- A section continuing across a page break repeats its header ("URINE ROUTINE (contd.)"), so every page shows its specimen context.
- Headers repeat on every page.
- No GT analyte key is repeated within one report and specimen.

#### 3.2.1 Shared-family feature mix (cross-cutting, seeded per report)

- Footers on every template: "Page x of y", end-of-report line, phone, toll-free, turnaround time, accreditation-certificate line (no real body named), address, signature with registration number. All are GT decoys.
- Patient block with age/sex, IDs, lab number and dates (decoys).
- Qualifiers on about 3% of eligible rows (§3.6).
- SI units on about 30% of generic reports (UK-A always SI, US-A always conventional).
- Indian digit grouping "2,50,000" in IN-* families; "250,000" in US-A.
- Range syntax variants: "a - b", "a–b", "a to b", "(a-b)", "[a - b]", "< b", "Up to b", "> a", and sex- or age-labelled forms.
- Printing restrictions with the standard 14 fonts: "≤" and "≥" are printed as "<=" and ">=". µ, ³, × and – are allowed (WinAnsi).

#### 3.2.2 Heldout-only families: constraints only

The plan deliberately does **not** specify them. Task T08 designs three families that:
1. are not cosmetic variants of a shared family;
2. use only features present in dev/test;
3. each differ from every shared family in at least two of: text-layer order, range syntax, unit placement, flag placement, column count and order, font family;
4. include 5–10 distinctive **canary tokens** each (fictional lab names, distinctive header words), listed in `tools/eval/layouts/heldout/families.py` as `CANARY_TOKENS`.

Their structural summary lives only in `tools/eval/layouts/heldout/README.md`, which opens with:

> DO NOT READ when working on extraction, the engine or templates (FR-5.4). Reading this folder or `eval/corpus/heldout/` to write rules invalidates the heldout metric.

Engine-builder prompts in P3 must repeat this. GN-12 fails if any canary token appears in `app/`.

### 3.4 Analyte catalogue (`catalogue.py`)

Fields per analyte:
- `key` (registry key namespace; new keys for specimen-specific analytes, below);
- `display`;
- printed-name variants by locale (IN, US, UK, generic);
- `specimen` (`serum` means any blood specimen: serum, plasma or whole blood);
- `value_type` (`numeric | qualitative | titre | range_result | info`);
- `key_optional` (True for qualitative and info analytes);
- conventional unit and its spellings;
- SI unit with `factor` (conventional × factor = SI) and decimals for each system;
- plausibility limits;
- reference variants (adult unisex or by sex; paediatric bands where supported);
- `can_qualify`;
- `derived` formula;
- Table C entry or cap;
- panel membership.

### 3.5 Table C — independent critical values (`critical_table.py`)

**Basis.** Thresholds follow consensus critical-value lists: Kost GJ, JAMA 1990;263:704-7, US institutional means (approximately K 2.8/6.2, Na 120/158, glucose 46/484 mg/dL, Ca 6.6/12.9 mg/dL, iCa 0.78/1.58, Mg 1.0/4.7, P 1.1, Hb 6.6/19.9, platelets 37k/910k, WBC 2.0k/37k, pH 7.21/7.59, pCO2 19/67, pO2 43, total CO2 11/40); later institutional surveys (Dighe 2006; CAP Q-Probes) show the spread between institutions.

**Guard bands** cover that legitimate spread. The generator **never emits** a value inside a guard band, so `is_critical` is the same under any reasonable institutional policy. Engine limits were not used to set the table; they were only checked afterwards (§1 fact 7).

**Semantics.** For blood specimens only (`specimen == serum`), in canonical units after converting the printed value:
- low side: `is_critical` when v < `low_guard[0]`; non-critical low values are > `low_guard[1]`;
- high side: non-critical high values are < `high_guard[0]`; `is_critical` when v > `high_guard[1]`.

Non-blood specimens are never critical (S5).

### 3.6 Value sampling (`scenarios.py`)

All states are relative to the patient's **effective printed range** [lo, hi], or to the consensus interval when no range is printed:
- **normal**: uniform in [lo + 0.1w, hi − 0.1w], where w = hi − lo. For a one-sided "< b": [0.4b, 0.9b]. For "> a": [1.15a, 2a].
- **mild**: within 3–25% beyond the bound (high: [1.03·hi, 1.25·hi]; low: [0.80·lo, 0.97·lo]).
- **severe**: 25–200% beyond the bound, clipped to stay outside the guard bands and non-critical.
- **critical**: from the Table C emitted-critical ranges.

Hard invariants, re-checked after converting to the printed unit, rounding (Decimal ROUND_HALF_UP to the printed decimals) and converting back:
- never inside a guard band;
- never equal to a printed bound (at least one display step away);
- within plausibility limits.

On a violation, resample up to 50 times; then drop the row from the report and record a generator warning. `generate --verify` fails if more than 0.5% of rows are dropped.

**Range-absent rows** occur only for adults with known age (§3.8). They use clear margins: normal values sit in the middle 60% of the consensus interval; abnormal values lie at least 15% beyond the bound furthest out across *all* catalogue variants. Any reasonable interval then gives the same flag. Interpretive-only rows follow the same rule against the category boundaries.

**Derived values** are computed from primaries and checked against the same invariants:
- indirect bilirubin = total − direct; globulin = TP − Alb; A/G ratio;
- VLDL = TG/5; LDL by Friedewald when TG < 400, otherwise a "Not calculated" decoy and no LDL row;
- non-HDL; the cholesterol/HDL and LDL/HDL ratios;
- MCHC, MCH and MCV from Hb, Hct and RBC (within ±1%);
- absolute counts = WBC × %/100;
- eGFR by CKD-EPI 2021.

**Qualifiers** apply only to analytes without a Table C entry (TSH, CRP, ferritin, PSA, procalcitonin, D-dimer, B12, troponin "< 0.01" normal only). The qualified interval must lie entirely within one flag region, for example "< 0.01" against 0.4–4.0 is LOW and "> 1650" against 11–307 is HIGH.

### 4.2 Derivation rules (`clinical.py`, pure functions)

1. **Effective printed range.** A two-sided or one-sided range is used as printed. For sex or age options, take the option matching the *input* sex or age. If the input is unknown, the range is null unless the value is on the same side of every option. Interpretive or category-only ranges give null; the flag is then computed against the consensus interval with clear margins (§3.6). A range unit always equals the result unit (generator rule).
2. **`flag`** (page). Compare the printed value, with its qualifier semantics, to `effective_range_page`. If null and the case is AMB, the flag is UNKNOWN. If null for a range-absent adult row with known demographics, compare to the consensus interval. If `is_critical`, the flag is CRITICAL_* in the value's direction. AMB_UNITLESS_COUNT always gives UNKNOWN, even when the true value is critical. Qualitative: NEGATIVE for Nil, Negative, Absent, Not detected, Non-Reactive and urobilinogen "Normal"; POSITIVE for Trace, 1+ to 4+, Present, Positive, Reactive and Detected. Info rows are INFO. A titre with a printed cut-off is POSITIVE if ≥ the cut-off, otherwise NEGATIVE.
3. **`flag_true`.** The same comparison using `unit_true`, `effective_range_true` and the current value.
4. **`is_critical`.** Table C on `canonical.value`, computed from the true value and unit, blood specimens only.
5. **Headline.** For a job (a whole report, or one page for photos): critical if any row is critical; otherwise abnormal if any `flag_true` is HIGH, LOW or POSITIVE; otherwise normal. "High" versus "moderate" is deliberately not judged, because no clinically derivable ground truth exists for the engine's severity tiers (S7 semantics).
6. GT is never edited by hand. A GT bug is fixed in the generator; the change is logged in DECISIONS and the corpus is re-baselined.

### 4.3 Manifest and fingerprint

`eval/corpus/<split>/manifest.json` holds:
- the schema;
- the split fingerprint;
- seeds and counts;
- `reports[]`: `{id, family, template_id, scenario_class, n_pages, rot90, is_csv, files{rendering: {path, sha256}}}`.

The split fingerprint is SHA-256 over:
- the sorted (module path, source SHA-256) pairs of the GT-side and render modules that the split uses (heldout-only modules only in the heldout fingerprint);
- the seeds and N;
- the library versions (reportlab, PyMuPDF, Pillow, numpy);
- the schema versions.

`materialize` skips any file whose recorded SHA-256 and fingerprint match.

## 5. Renderings (`render.py`)

| Rendering | Recipe (all parameters seeded per report and rendering, recorded in `render_meta`) |
|---|---|
| **native** | reportlab Canvas with `invariant=1`, standard-14 fonts only (Helvetica, Times, Courier; nothing embedded), 7.5–10 pt; A4 or Letter as per the family. Text-layer order as per the family table, which matters because `get_text("text")` follows content order. |
| **csv** | GEN-CSV only: UTF-8 text in the chosen dialect. No image renderings. |
| **scan** | PyMuPDF rasterises the native PDF at **150 dpi**, grayscale. Then, numpy + Pillow only: skew ±1.0°; brightness and contrast ±5%; additive Gaussian noise σ 2–6 grey levels; speckle density 0.0002–0.001; blur σ 0–0.6; a faint streak in 10% of pages; JPEG round-trip at q 70–90 (Pillow). The image goes into a new PDF page of the original size, with no text layer. Saved via PyMuPDF with fixed metadata dates and `no_new_id=True`. If the bytes prove unstable, the determinism test compares decoded pixmaps instead (record in DECISIONS). |
| **photo** (one PNG per page; one job per page) | Rasterise at 200 dpi RGB and place on a desk-texture background (page ≈ 85% of the frame). Perspective comes from a pinhole homography computed with numpy: out-of-plane tilts θx, θy uniform in [−8°, 8°] (the PRD's ≤ 8°), in-plane rotation ±3°, focal length 1.0–1.3 × frame width; warped with Pillow PERSPECTIVE, bicubic. Uneven light: linear gradient (minimum factor 0.65–0.9) × vignette (0.8–1.0), plus a soft shadow band in 30% of photos and a colour cast ±5%. Gaussian blur σ uniform in [0, 1.2] (the PRD's ≤ 1.2). Sensor noise σ 1–4. Resize to a **1800 px long side** (messaging-app size, ≈150–155 dpi for A4). JPEG round-trip q uniform in [60, 85] (Pillow). Save PNG. Must be under 10 MB (validator limit). The homography is stored for P2 bbox checks. |
| **rot90** (5% of non-CSV reports) | The scan rendering's page images rotated +90° or −90° (seeded), on landscape-sized pages with no text layer. |

Validity checks:
- `verify` asserts every parameter is within its bounds.
- A **legibility diagnostic** reports the share of GT `value_text` strings that appear in the text the pipeline actually saw (§6.8).
- If a rendering looks broken (legibility < 99% native, < 95% scan, < 85% photo on a dev sample), inspect it. Fix only real rendering bugs (wrong resolution, clipping, inversion). **Never** narrow the degradations below the PRD maxima to raise scores. Record the finding.

- `pre_review_headline`: critical, abnormal, normal or unknown
- `product_label` (raw)
- `raw_text` (the text the pipeline saw; used for legibility only)
- `adapter`, `adapter_version`
- `meta` (OCR methods, cache hits)
- `timing` (non-deterministic)

**Protocols**:
- `ExtractionAdapter`: `name`, `version`, `extract(job) -> ExtractionResult`.
- `Scorer`: `name`, `version`, `score(rows: list[ConfirmedRow], sex, age) -> ScoreResult`.

**`ConfirmedRow`** carries the same value fields as `ExtractedRow`, plus `origin` (accepted, edited or added) and `payload`. **`ScoreResult`**: `headline` (critical, abnormal, normal or unknown), `row_flags[]`, `label_raw`.

Selection is by `--adapter` (default `pipeline_v0` in P1) and `--scorer` (default `engine_floor_v0`).

**Changing the adapter or scorer never changes GT, the matching or the metric definitions.** It does invalidate `eval/baseline.json`; re-baseline and log a DECISIONS entry.

#### 6.3.2 Call path

For each job:
1. Install spies:

4. Build `ExtractedRow`s from `captured_analysis.rows`, which are exactly the rows `_scored_to_row` turns into `lab_values`:
   - `value_num`, `qualifier`, `text_value` (or `result_range` as "a-b") and `unit_raw` come from `row.parsed`;
   - `ref_low` and `ref_high` are the effective printed range from `row.parsed`: `ref_low`/`ref_high`, otherwise `sex_ranges[input sex]`, otherwise the matching `age_ranges` band, otherwise null;

   - `specimen` is urine, csf or fluid when `parsed.section` contains those words or the key starts `urine_`/`csf_` (or is `specific_gravity`), otherwise serum;
   - `payload` is the `ParsedRow`.
5. Urine path, mirroring the product (decided from the response, not re-derived). Engine dicts carry `range_source` and `analyte_key`; legacy urine dicts from `_urine_entities_to_rows` carry `section` and neither of those keys.
   - If `response.lab_values` holds only legacy dicts (urine-only branch), build rows from them with `origin=legacy_urine`.
   - If legacy dicts follow the engine rows (merged branch), append them as `legacy_urine`.
6. Legacy key map: ph → urine_ph, specific_gravity, protein → urine_protein, glucose → urine_glucose, ketones → urine_ketones, blood → urine_blood, nitrite → urine_nitrite, leukocyte_esterase → urine_leukocyte_esterase, bilirubin → urine_bilirubin, urobilinogen → urine_urobilinogen, wbc → urine_pus_cells, rbc → urine_rbc, epithelial_cells → urine_epithelial, *_casts → urine_casts, bacteria → urine_bacteria.
7. Legacy flags: for qualitative GT, ABNORMAL → POSITIVE and NORMAL → NEGATIVE; for numeric GT, ABNORMAL → "ABNORMAL" (direction unknown, so it never equals HIGH or LOW) and NORMAL → NORMAL.
8. `pre_review_headline` = map(`response.risk_label`, lower-cased): critical → critical; high, moderate, medium → abnormal; low, normal → normal; unknown or None → unknown. This is the product label including the ensemble (M5-pre and M6).
9. `status`: failed if `response.status != "completed"`; empty if there are no rows; otherwise ok.
10. `review_all` is true when the status is failed, or when `lab_values` is empty and the response carries the "Insufficient evidence … review it manually" message (pipeline.py:1560-1594).
11. Report-level review items: `analysis.warnings` not attributed to any row (below), and "N unread numeric line(s)" when `analysis.unparsed_numeric_lines > 0`. The pipeline's "Patient sex/age not provided" note is not in `analysis.warnings` and is not a review item.

#### 6.3.3 v0 needs_review rules

These are derived only from signals the current product shows the clinician.

| Code | Condition (r = ScoredRow, p = r.parsed) | Reason text |
|---|---|---|
| NR0-UNKNOWN | `r.flag == "UNKNOWN"` | "Not assessed" + attributed warning |
| NR0-VALUETYPE | `r.value_type` in {ambiguous, unit_mismatch, invalid} | value_type |
| NR0-NOANALYTE | `r.analyte_key is None` and `r.value_type` in {numeric, qualified} | "Unknown test" |
| NR0-FLAGCONFLICT | `r.discrepancy` is set | the discrepancy |
| NR0-WARNING | `p.warnings` is non-empty, or some `w` in `analysis.warnings` starts with `r.display_name + ":"` (attributed to every row of that name) | the warning |
| NR0-CRITICAL | `r.flag` in {CRITICAL_HIGH, CRITICAL_LOW} | "Looks critical — please verify". The product puts every critical in a banner, so an error there is a loud false alarm (M4), not a silent one. |

Legacy urine rows are `needs_review = False`: the product shows them with no uncertainty signal (N12).

Deliberately **not** in v0, because the current product does not compute or surface them:
- per-token OCR confidence < 0.90;
- column-parser origin;
- the 0.1×–10× plausibility check;
- unread lines as rows (they are counts only, so v0 never covers an omission).

P2 provides all four through the real `extracted_rows`.

### 6.4 Scorer v0 — `EngineFloorScorerV0`

Steps:
1. Build `ParsedRow`s: use the `payload` for accepted rows; build fresh ones for edited or added rows (name, value, qualifier, text, `unit_true`, range from `effective_range_true`, `printed_flag=None`, `section` = specimen header phrase).
2. Call `analyze_lab_rows(rows, sex, age)`, then `apply_risk_floor(analysis, None, None)`.
3. Apply the critical ceiling: "critical" without a critical row becomes high.
4. Map the label with the same function as above.

Limitation, documented in report.md: the ensemble can raise a no-anomaly report to abnormal, and v0 does not model that. The effect is visible in M5-pre. P2 must replace this scorer with the real confirmed-rows scoring (FR-1.4).

The `ClinicalRulesScorer` in `clinical.py` (GT rules) is used only in metric unit tests and for M5-oracle-clinical.

### 6.5 Review simulator (conservative clinician)

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| matched row, `needs_review` | **edited**: replaced by GT true values (name, key, value, qualifier, text, `unit_true`, `effective_range_true`, specimen) |
| matched row, not flagged | **accepted** as extracted, wrong or not |
| unmatched extracted row, flagged | **deleted** |
| unmatched extracted row, not flagged | **accepted**: the invented row persists |
| `unread_line` covering a GT row that has no match | **added** from GT true values |
| `unread_line` covering nothing | dismissed |
| GT row unmatched and not covered | missing: the clinician does not notice |
| `review_all` job | nothing added; the confirmed set is empty, so the headline is unknown |

### 6.6 Row matching (`match.align`)

1. **Normalise names**: lower-case; punctuation to spaces; spelling map (haemo → hemo, leuco → leuko, colour → color, oe → e in "oedema"-type words); drop noise tokens {serum, plasma, blood, level, test, count, value, result, s, sr}.
2. **Candidate pairs (g, e)**, with name cost:
   - **K**: `e.analyte_key == g.analyte_key` (non-null), cost 0;
   - **N**: otherwise token Jaccard ≥ 0.5, cost 1 − J;
   - **T**: otherwise g's name tokens ⊆ tokens of `e.name_raw` ∪ `e.source_text`, with J ≥ 0.25, cost 0.8 (handles glued names such as "Potassium 4.2 5.1").
3. **Value cost**: 0 if equal under the value comparator; 0.3 if within 10%; 0.6 otherwise. For qualitative rows: 0 if the same class, otherwise 0.6.
4. **Order cost**: 0.01 × |rank_g − rank_e| / max(n_g, n_e).
5. A pair is eligible if total cost ≤ 1.2.
6. **Assignment**: greedy over eligible pairs, stably sorted by (cost, g.index, e.index). A pair is taken when both sides are free. GT keys are unique per report-specimen, so greedy equals optimal except in contrived ties; no scipy.
7. **Unread-line coverage**, for GT rows with no match: `u` covers `g` if `u.source_text`, normalised, contains `g.value_text` as a token and at least one non-noise name token of `g`. One line may cover several rows (two per line).
8. Photo jobs align only the rows on that page.

### 6.7 What counts as wrong (`match` comparators, `units.py`)

A matched pair is **correct** only if `name_ok ∧ value_ok ∧ unit_ok ∧ range_ok ∧ flag_ok`.

- **name_ok**: (`g.analyte_key` is not null and `e.analyte_key == g.analyte_key`), or (`g.key_optional` and `e.analyte_key` ∈ {None, g.analyte_key} and name Jaccard ≥ 0.5). Specimen is part of the key: `sodium` is not `urine_sodium`.
- **value_ok**:
  - numeric: equal qualifier (None ≠ "<"; "<" ≠ "<="), and either |e − g| ≤ 1e-9 + 1e-6·|g| in printed space, or the (value, unit) pairs are equal in canonical units within 1e-4 relative. Digit grouping is normalised, so "2,50,000" equals 250000.
  - qualitative: same class and grade ("++" ≡ "2+"; "Trace" ≢ "1+"; Nil ≡ Negative for dipstick).
  - info: normalised text equal.
  - titre: ratio equal.
  - range_result: both bounds equal.
- **unit_ok**: same canonical token in `units.py`, or both empty or None, or the canonical-pair equivalence above holds. Not applicable (true) for qualitative, info and titre rows. The canonical token classes:
  - g/dL ≡ gm/dL ≡ g% ≡ gm%;
  - mg/dL ≡ mg%;
  - µmol/L ≡ umol/L ≡ μmol/L;
  - 10³/µL ≡ x10^3/uL ≡ ×10³/µL ≡ K/µL ≡ thou/mm3 ≡ 10^9/L ≡ x10^9/L ≡ G/L;
  - /µL ≡ /uL ≡ cells/µL ≡ /cumm ≡ cells/cumm ≡ /mm3 ≡ /cmm;
  - million/µL ≡ mill/cumm ≡ 10^6/µL ≡ 10^12/L;
  - lakh/cumm ≡ lakhs/cumm;
  - U/L ≡ IU/L;
  - µIU/mL ≡ uIU/mL ≡ mIU/L;
  - ng/mL ≡ µg/L;
  - pg/mL ≡ ng/L;
  - µg/dL ≡ ug/dL ≡ mcg/dL;
  - mm/hr ≡ mm/1st hr ≡ mm/h;
  - s ≡ sec;
  - /hpf ≡ /HPF ≡ cells/hpf.
  
  These stay distinct: mEq/L ≢ mmol/L, and mIU/mL ≢ mIU/L.
- **range_ok**: both effective ranges null, or both present with each bound equal within 1e-6 relative (or canonical-equivalent with the unit) and the same sides present. Strictness is ignored, because boundary values are never emitted. Not applicable (true) for qualitative, info and titre rows.
- **flag_ok**: `e.flag == g.flag` (page flag). Legacy ABNORMAL never equals HIGH or LOW.

The error signature is the set of failed fields. It is used for diagnostics only.

### 6.8 Metric definitions (per split × rendering cell; all counts additive)

Notation: for job j, `G_j` are the GT rows, `E_j` the extracted rows of kind `row`, `U_j` the unread-line items, `A_j` the matched pairs, `C_j` the covered GT rows, and `nr` = needs_review.

| Metric | Numerator / denominator |
|---|---|
| **M1 silent-error rate** | Numerator: \|{(g,e) ∈ A: ¬correct ∧ ¬e.nr}\| + \|{e ∈ E unmatched: ¬e.nr}\| + (`review_all_j` ? 0 : \|{g unmatched ∧ g ∉ C}\|). Denominator: Σ\|G_j\|. Components are reported as M1.wrong, M1.invented, M1.omitted. Omissions and inventions count; this is the conservative reading. |
| **M2 critical recall** | Numerator: GT critical rows g with (g matched to e and (e.flag ∈ CRITICAL_* in either direction, or e.nr)) or g ∈ C. Denominator: all GT critical rows. A `review_all` job does **not** cover criticals. n/a when the denominator is 0. |
| **M3 row accuracy before review** | Numerator: correct pairs. Denominator: Σ(\|G_j\| + \|unmatched E_j\|), so inventions count against accuracy. Recall, precision and per-field accuracies are reported alongside. |
| **M4 false-critical rate** | Numerator: extracted rows with flag CRITICAL_* that are unmatched or whose GT row is not critical. Denominator: Σ\|G_j\|. Measured before review. |
| **M5 headline on confirmed** | Numerator: jobs where `scorer.score(review_sim(j)).headline == GT headline`. Denominator: all jobs. |
| **M6 normal → ready** | Denominator J_N: jobs with GT headline normal and an empty expected-needs_review set. Numerator: jobs in J_N with status ok, \|E\| > 0, no e.nr, no U, no report-level items, and `pre_review_headline == normal`. |
| **M7 review burden** | Numerator: \|{x ∈ E ∪ U: x.nr}\|. Denominator: \|E ∪ U\|. |

Diagnostics (non-gating):
- **M2-post**: GT criticals that are CRITICAL after review and scoring.
- **M5-pre**: the product's pre-review headline against GT.
- **M5-oracle**: all GT rows corrected, then scored by the active scorer (isolates scoring errors such as N3 or the INR limit). As a self-consistency check, `ClinicalRulesScorer` on GT rows must reproduce the GT headline for 100% of jobs; `verify` fails otherwise.
- **R-NR**: expected-needs_review recall (AMB rows matched and flagged ÷ AMB rows).
- **Legibility**: GT `value_text` found as a token in `raw_text`.
- Recall and precision.
- Invented rows by decoy kind (token overlap ≥ 0.5 with a decoy); omissions by GT feature.
- Confusion matrix of headlines; per-family M3; top 20 error signatures.
- Crash counts by exception class; runtime p50 and p95 per rendering.

Wilson 95% intervals are reported for every rate. All diagnostics except crash counts, legibility and the M1 components are reported for dev and test only (§6.11).

### 6.9 Targets (`targets.py`, immutable) and gate modes

The values are PRD §3, applied per rendering.

| Metric | native | csv | scan | rot90 | photo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 ≤ | 0.5% | 0.5% | 1.5% | 1.5% | 1.5% |
| M2 = | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| M3 ≥ | 92% | 92% | 80% | 80% | 70% |
| M4 ≤ | 0.5% | 0.5% | 0.5% | 0.5% | 0.5% |
| M5 ≥ | 98% | 98% | 98% | 98% | 98% |
| M6 ≥ | 90% | 90% | info | info | info |
| M7 ≤ | 15% | 15% | info | info | info |

CL-31 asserts that these numbers equal the PRD, so weakening them requires visibly editing a test.

| Mode | Behaviour | Used |
|---|---|---|
| `none` | compute and write only | exploration, dev tuning |
| `report` | evaluate targets and print PASS/FAIL; exit 0 unless the harness errors | full and heldout runs (P1–P6) |
| `ratchet` | test split, CI profile: none of **M1–M5** may regress beyond tolerance versus `eval/baseline.json`, and the crash count may not rise | **P1–P2 CI gate** |
| `targets` | all targets on the test split, plus ratchet | from the P3 gate |

M6 and M7 are **never ratcheted**. More review flags are a legitimate safety trade-off (M1 down, M7 up), so M6 and M7 are checked only against their targets, in targets mode.

Ratchet tolerance, as counts: 0 for native and csv; 1 for scan, photo and rot90 (an allowance for OCR differences between CPUs when the OCR cache is cold). A corpus fingerprint, profile, adapter or scorer that differs from the baseline exits 2 with instructions. The baseline is never silently reused.

Accepting a deliberate trade-off that lowers a ratcheted metric requires `make eval-baseline` plus a DECISIONS line naming the metrics that moved and why.

`eval/gate.json` = `{mode: "ratchet", profile: "ci", split: "test", baseline: "eval/baseline.json", tolerance: {native: 0, csv: 0, scan: 1, photo: 1, rot90: 1}}`. The P3 gate flips the mode to `targets`.

Exit codes: 0 pass; 1 gate failed; 2 harness or config error (missing baseline or corpus, fingerprint mismatch, import failure); 3 hygiene violation. Output files are written atomically (temp file, then rename), so a failed run leaves no partial `metrics.json`.

### 6.10 Outputs

- `results[split][rendering]`: per metric `{num, den, value, ci_low, ci_high, target, pass}`, plus `components`;

- `gate`: mode, passed, failures[];

**`eval/report.md`**, deterministic body:

4. The M1 components.

### 6.11 Heldout hygiene guard (`hygiene.py`)

1. **One choke point.** Every per-report output (failure dumps, job records written to disk, debug JSON, shard job records) goes through `guard_per_report_output(split)`, which raises `HygieneError` for heldout. The CLI rejects `--dump-failures` with heldout before doing any work (exit 3).
2. **Whitelist redaction.** The heldout results go through `redact_heldout(cell)`, which keeps only, per rendering: M1–M7 `{num, den, value, ci_low, ci_high, target, pass}`, M1 component counts, legibility, the job count and crash counts by exception class name. Any other key raises. There is no per-family, per-analyte, per-job or confusion breakdown for heldout.
3. **Quiet mode.** Heldout jobs run inside `quiet()`: app loggers are disabled and stdout/stderr go to `os.devnull` during each job. Exceptions are recorded as the class name only; no message, no traceback.
4. **No serialisation.** Heldout job records stay in memory, cross the worker-to-parent boundary only as additive counts, and are never written. Heldout shards emit redacted cells only.
5. **Fixed template.** The report.md heldout section is rendered from the redacted dict through a fixed template. HA-06 asserts that no heldout report ID (`H\d{3}`) or GT analyte name appears in report.md, metrics.json or captured output.
6. **Git hygiene.** No heldout corpus file is tracked: a test asserts `git ls-files eval/corpus` is empty. Golden samples are dev only.
7. **Code hygiene.** The heldout-only family code is behind the DO-NOT-READ notice, and the GN-12 canary test guards `app/`.

### 6.12 OCR cache, profiles, sampling, parallelism, runtime

**OCR cache.** `ocr_cache` wraps `ocr_service.ocr_image`.
- Key: SHA-256(preprocessed image bytes, shape, dtype) + fingerprint. The fingerprint covers SHA-256 of `data/paddleocr/**`, the paddleocr and paddlepaddle versions, SHA-256 of `app/services/ocr_service.py`, and a schema salt.
- Value: the list of `{text, confidence, bbox}` as JSON, stored in `eval/.cache/ocr/<2-hex>/<key>.json`.
- `--ocr-cache off` bypasses it.
- Everything around Paddle (CLAHE, `lines_from_boxes`, the native-versus-OCR choice) still runs live, so the cache never hides a product change.

**Profiles** (`config.py`; `--profile`, overridable with `--splits`, `--renderings`, `--sample k=v,...`, `--sample-n N`):

| Profile | Splits | native / csv | scan | photo | rot90 | Gate | Failure dumps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ci` (= `make eval`) | test | 100% | 25% | 25% (same reports as scan) | 100% | eval/gate.json | off |
| `dev` | dev | 100% | 25% | 25% | 100% | none | eval/failures/dev/ |
| `full` | dev, test, heldout | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | report | off |
| `heldout` | heldout | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | report | forbidden |
| `smoke` | test, first 6 by stable order | 100% | — | — | — | none | off |

**Sampling.** Stratified by scenario class. Within each class, reports are ranked by SHA-256(`report_id|img-sample-v1`) and the first round(p × n) are taken. The choice is stable and independent of the other reports in the manifest.

**Parallelism.** `--jobs N`, default 1, uses a spawn-context pool. Each worker sets up the hermetic environment, spies and cache once. Results are sorted by `job_id` before aggregation. `--shard k/n` deterministically partitions a cell's jobs. `run aggregate --in DIR` merges shard counts.

**Runtime budget**, from the measured 2.9 s per OCR page (native pages included), 0.7 s per job and a mean of 1.7 pages; CI profile, 4-vCPU reference container, cold caches:

| Item | Jobs | OCR pages | Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| test native + csv | 76 + 4 | ≈129 | ≈7.8 min |
| test scan 25% | 19 | ≈32 | ≈1.9 min |
| test photo 25% | ≈32 page jobs | 32 | ≈1.9 min |
| test rot90 | 4 | ≈7 | ≈0.4 min |
| generation of test-split renderings + startup | — | — | ≈1.3 min |
| **Total** | ≈135 | ≈200 | **≈13 min** (≤ 20 min budget). Warm OCR cache: ≈3 min. A 2-vCPU runner, cold: ≈20–22 min (job timeout 45). |

Full run, cold: ≈1,443 jobs and ≈1,970 OCR pages, ≈2 h on one 4-vCPU process. In CI it is sharded into 15 cells; the longest shard (dev native or dev photo) takes ≈35–40 min on 2 vCPU. Disk: ≈3 GB for the full corpus (photos dominate), all git-ignored.

## 8. Determinism and reproducibility

- **Generator**: per-purpose hashed seeds (§3.1); sorted iteration everywhere; Decimal rounding; reportlab `invariant=1` with standard fonts; PyMuPDF saves with fixed metadata and `no_new_id`; Pillow + numpy degradations (no cv2 at generation time); canonical JSON.
- **Asserted by tests**:
  - GN-04: same seed gives byte-identical GT, native, scan, photo, rot90 and csv files within one environment;
  - GN-06: GT JSON and native PDF bytes match the committed golden SHA-256s across environments (pinned reportlab and PyMuPDF);
  - GN-05: a different seed gives a different corpus;
  - HA-07: two harness runs give byte-identical `metrics.json` deterministic sections;
  - HA-08: sampling is stable.
- **Scope**: OCR'd renderings are reproducible on the same CPU, paddle build and OCR cache. Across CPUs with a cold cache, small OCR differences are possible; this is the reason for the ratchet tolerance of 1 count on image renderings. "Same seed, same metrics" (E5 AC) holds exactly for native and csv, and for image renderings given the OCR cache.
- **Change control**: any generator change after the P1 gate changes the split fingerprint, so ratchet exits 2. It requires a DECISIONS entry, a re-baseline in the same commit, and never removes a failing case.

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| OCR differs between CPUs, so the ratchet flakes | OCR cache in CI; tolerance of 1 count on image renderings; native and csv exact |
| A cold `eval-gate` on a 2-vCPU runner takes ≈ 20–22 min | 45-minute timeout; warm cache ≈ 3 min; the ≤ 20 min budget is defined on the 4-vCPU reference |
| The generator has a bug, so the GT is wrong | single `PrintedRow` source for page and GT; bbox-text test; `--verify` invariants; zoro review; clinical tests from Table C |
| The synthetic corpus is easier than real reports (PRD §12) | heldout-only families; degradations at the PRD maxima; text-layer order variants; decoys; record the limitation |
| Guard bands remove the debatable zones (realism lost there) | documented; the zones are where critical policy differs, so their absence makes GT policy-independent |
| The spy adapter breaks when P2 refactors the pipeline | HA-02 fidelity test; v0 is retired at P2, and P1 numbers remain in git history |
| Heldout leakage through code reading | DO-NOT-READ folder; constraints-only plan text; canary test; quiet mode; whitelist redaction |
| Disk (≈ 3 GB) or CI cache size | image renderings are not cached in CI; only GT, native and csv plus the OCR JSON |

## 15. Decisions to record in docs/DECISIONS.md

1. P1 changes no product code; the harness observes `run_report_pipeline` via spies and stubs (`PipelineV0Adapter`). P1 measures the offline, no-LLM configuration (no Groq; RAG and the urinalysis KB stubbed; `_call_groq` fails fast).
2. The v0 needs_review rules are NR0-UNKNOWN, NR0-VALUETYPE, NR0-NOANALYTE, NR0-FLAGCONFLICT, NR0-WARNING and NR0-CRITICAL. Per-row OCR confidence, column-parser origin and unread-line rows are absent in v0, because the current product does not surface them; they arrive with P2.
3. M1 counts silent inventions and silent omissions as well as wrong rows; the M3 denominator includes unmatched extracted rows. This is the stricter, conservative reading of PRD §3.
4. A job where nothing was extracted and the product tells the clinician to review it manually covers omissions for M1 but **not** for M2.
5. The headline is judged on three levels (critical, abnormal, normal); "high" versus "moderate" is not judged (no independent GT exists).
6. M5 uses a conservative simulated clinician and the v0 engine-floor scorer (ensemble not modelled). M5-pre and M5-oracle are reported. P2 swaps in the real scorer.
7. The M6 denominator excludes reports with genuinely ambiguous rows. M6 and M7 are gated on native and csv only. rot90 takes the scan thresholds and csv takes the native ones.
8. Table C: independent critical values (Kost 1990 consensus) with guard bands covering institutional variation; blood specimens only. The generator never emits values in a guard band or exactly at a printed bound. Caps apply where there is no consensus (bilirubin, TG, Hct, aPTT/PT, CK, ALT/AST, amylase/lipase, uric acid, vitamin D, CSF glucose < 20). The engine's INR > 3.0 limit and its missing limits for high Hb, pCO2, pO2 and HCO3 are expected P3 findings; Table C is never edited to match the engine.
9. Paediatric ages are 2–17 only (no neonates); paediatric reports always print age-appropriate ranges. Range-absent rows only occur for adults of known age, with clear-margin values.
10. Trace dipstick counts as POSITIVE. CSF differential, urine colour, appearance and crystals, and blood group are INFO. The specimen value "serum" means any blood specimen (FR-1.2 enum).
